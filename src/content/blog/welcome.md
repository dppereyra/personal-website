# Welcome!

Hello, and thanks for stopping by.

I'm Dennis — an SRE / DevOps / platform engineer. If you've landed here from a search
result for some obscure error string, I hope the post you came for saved you an
afternoon. If you're just looking around, here's who's writing.

## How I think about problems

I'm a strong advocate of information-based planning and decision making. I like
dealing with problems analytically: gather what's actually true, then decide — rather
than deciding first and collecting evidence to match.

Two beliefs follow from that, and they pull in opposite directions often enough to be
interesting:

- **Don't fix a working system.** Change has a cost, and "it could be cleaner" is not
  on its own a reason to touch something that works.
- **Use what you have to its full potential.** Most tools are underused rather than
  inadequate. Before reaching for a new one, it's worth asking whether the current one
  was ever pushed.

I also think most software problems are solvable. It's rare for something to be
genuinely blocked by a system limitation — far more often the problem just hasn't been
looked at from the right angle yet. That's usually a prompt to change perspective, not
to give up.

On languages: Python is mine, and I think real depth in one language matters. But
staying language-agnostic is what shows you the range of possible solutions to a
problem. The second language is where you learn that your first one had opinions.

## What I work on

**Infrastructure.** I'm part of a start-up, where I handle the IT side: choosing the
software, designing the overall infrastructure, and then living with those decisions
by managing and maintaining it. Owning the consequences of your own architecture is
an excellent teacher.

**Data.** I care about clean, concise data and I'm realistic about how hard that is.
The problems start earlier than most people expect — not at processing, but at
deciding what to collect in the first place — and run all the way through cleaning,
storage, processing, and reporting.

**Whatever's new.** I like keeping up with where things are going, though mostly
through a practical lens: can this actually be used, for my own ventures or at work?

## Away from the keyboard

I mod games. There's something satisfying about getting under the hood and tinkering
with the files just to add a personal touch to something I'm already enjoying — which
is, now that I write it down, not that different from what I do professionally.

I read a lot: technology books, self-help, and mystery, fantasy, and science fiction
when I want to be somewhere else for a while. I'm also an anime and manga enthusiast,
light novels included.

## What to expect here

Mostly write-ups of things I've debugged or built, with the reasoning left in —
including the parts where I was wrong, because that's usually the useful bit. Some
slide decks too.

Posts and decks written with AI help carry a visible marker, so you always know what
you're reading.

Have a look around.
